Contrast is stretched to each frame's own range, which is the only way the cells stay visible: the drift term is common to every pixel and grows to RU, several times the binding response itself. The panel opposite shows what happens without that stretch.

On an absolute scale the surface washes out as drift accumulates. The cells do not get brighter — everything does. This is the confound the simulator exists to reproduce, and the reason a cell-free reference region is normally subtracted.

Capacity field s[k]

Overlap resolved by a power diagram, so internal boundaries are straight radical axes rather than arcs. Brightness rises quadratically from 0.15× capacity at each cell's centre to full capacity at its free rim.

Gradient weight ĝ[k] = |∇s| / p95

Sobel magnitude, normalised to the 95th percentile of its non-zero values. This one field weights both the drift and the non-specific adsorption terms, concentrating each at cluster edges and leaving flat interiors and open background untouched.

The traces are cumulative: each band is one term of the assembly equation, stacked in the order it appears. The specific binding signal — the only part carrying kinetic information — is the thin band at the bottom.

Three pixels, as recorded

A bright rim pixel, a cell interior, and open background. All three rise together because drift dominates all three.

After subtracting the background pixel

Subtraction removes the spatially uniform part of the drift exactly. What survives is the gradient-weighted remainder — largest at the rim, where ĝ is largest — plus the binding curve. The residual drift is RU at the rim pixel.
