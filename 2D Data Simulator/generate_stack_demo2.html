What the stack is made ofbefore it is assembled.

Every pixel in the finished stack is four terms added together: two spatial fields fixed once at the start, and three time series shared by the whole frame. This page shows those objects on their own, in the form the interface would plot them.

Two spatial fields

Both length m·n, row-major, index k = i·n + j. Fixed after Phase 1.

s dimensionless, [0, 1] — capacity × edge profile
0 — background1.0 — full-capacity rim

Cells differ in brightness because they carry different capacity bins, and within each cell brightness rises from 0.15× at the centre to full at the free rim. The RU scale is not here — it lives in Ŷ. A pixel's specific-binding contribution is s[k] · Ŷ[t], peaking at RU.

w dimensionless, (0, 1] — edge proximity
0 — far from any margin

Distance to the cell/medium interface, falling off with λin = 8 px inward and λout = 2 px outward. Note every ring looks the same: w is independent of capacity, unlike s. That is deliberate — drift is an instrument phenomenon and must not scale with what a cell expresses.

Three time series

All length T, plotted against grid in seconds. Shaded bands show the range actually realised across the frame once each is spatially weighted.

Yhat[bin]  specific binding · RU

Integrated at Rmax = RU then normalised by the bin fraction, so it is already in RU and needs no rescaling. Weighted by s[k], which spans 0 to 1, so the band runs from the background (zero) up to this curve. Association is shaded; note the response never returns to baseline — there is no regeneration, so binding accumulates across the series.

Yns  non-specific adsorption dimensionless — multiply by nsaRate[k] for RU

The one object that is not in RU: integrated at Rns = 1, it is a fractional occupancy peaking at . The band brackets the whole NSA contribution — lower edge an·Yns on open background ( RU peak), upper edge (an+bn)·Yns at a cell margin ( RU peak). Note it keeps climbing while binding falls.

driftCommon  baseline drift · RU already RU — multiply by gDrift[k]

A saturating exponential (D = RU, τ = 500 s) with an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck wander that makes it unfittable by a smooth baseline model. The band is narrow — gDrift runs only 1.000 to — but it is the part a cell-free reference cannot remove, because the uniform component cancels and this does not. Drift reaches RU, several times the binding response.

Putting them back together

Frames are computed on demand — the stack is never stored here.

The assembled frame, jitter included. Because drift is common to every pixel, the contrast stretch removes most of it as a side effect — which is why the cells stay visible at all.

Each shaded band is one term of the assembly equation, and its thickness is that term's contribution — the curves themselves are running totals, not individual terms. Drift sits at the bottom because it is the largest and smoothest; specific binding is the thin blue band near the top, the only part carrying kinetic information. Compare its thickness against the drift band beneath it.
